# FashionPulse AI — Trend Momentum Methodology & Limitations

## 1. Mathematical Momentum Formulation

Trend momentum measures the velocity and acceleration of fashion expressions observed across structured social media content windows.

### A. Frequency & Share of Voice
Let $C_i$ be the number of observations mentioning trend $i$ in the current active observation window ($t \in [t_{\text{now}} - 7\text{d}, t_{\text{now}}]$), and let $N_{\text{curr}}$ be the total volume of fashion-validated records in that window ($N_{\text{curr}} = 149$):
$$f_i = \frac{C_i}{N_{\text{curr}}}$$

### B. Historical Comparison & Growth Rate
Let $B_i$ be the observation count in the baseline comparison window ($t \in [t_{\text{now}} - 14\text{d}, t_{\text{now}} - 7\text{d}]$), with total baseline fashion volume $N_{\text{base}}$ ($N_{\text{base}} = 12$):

* **Raw Growth Percentage:**
  $$\text{growth\_rate} = \frac{C_i - B_i}{B_i} \times 100\%$$
* **Share-of-Voice Growth Percentage (Collection-Effort Normalized):**
  $$\text{sov\_growth} = \frac{(C_i / N_{\text{curr}}) - (B_i / N_{\text{base}})}{B_i / N_{\text{base}}} \times 100\%$$

### C. Continuous Velocity Activation (Hyperbolic Tangent)
To eliminate linear saturation ceilings (where any growth $\ge 100\%$ previously clipped flatly to $100.0$), FashionPulse AI applies a smooth, continuous velocity response function:

$$\text{momentum\_score} = \begin{cases}
\min\left(99.0, \; 50.0 + 50.0 \times \tanh\left(\frac{\text{growth\_rate}}{S}\right)\right) & \text{if } \text{growth\_rate} \ge 0 \\
\max\left(5.0, \; 50.0 - 50.0 \times \tanh\left(\frac{|\text{growth\_rate}|}{S_{\text{neg}}}\right)\right) & \text{if } \text{growth\_rate} < 0
\end{cases}$$

Where:
* $S = 1200.0$ scales positive acceleration over wide percentage domains ($+100\%$ to $+2500\%$), preventing premature saturation and preserving distinct gradients across high-velocity trends.
* $S_{\text{neg}} = 400.0$ scales downward trajectory.
* A baseline growth of $0\%$ yields a neutral velocity of $50.0$.
* When a trend has zero baseline ($B_i = 0$), it is classified as `"emerging"` with a calibrated baseline momentum of $75.0$.

## 2. Investigation of Historical Baseline Validity

An empirical audit of the underlying database records revealed critical methodological asymmetries between the baseline and current observation windows:

1. **Search Keyword Configuration Shift:**
   * In initial ingestions, the connector searched only two broad queries (`fashion trends 2026`, `streetwear style`), yielding 40 videos where $58.3\%$ of the few baseline videos were streetwear.
   * In later runs, the search taxonomy expanded to 10 distinct categories (knitwear, footwear, denim, blazers, accessories, etc.), capturing 184 additional videos across diverse niches.
2. **Collection Frequency & Timing Asymmetry:**
   * Both windows ($[-7\text{d}, 0\text{d}]$ and $[-14\text{d}, -7\text{d}]$) were not generated by a continuous longitudinal daily ingestion schedule executed 14 days ago.
   * Instead, they represent an opportunistic retrospective sample obtained from search requests executed on October 28–29, 2026.
3. **Sample Size Disparity:**
   * YouTube's search relevance algorithm naturally favors recent uploads for active queries. Consequently, the current 7-day window contains 149 fashion-validated videos, whereas the 7–14 day window contains only 12 videos ($12.4\times$ volume difference).
4. **Conclusion on Baseline Validity:**
   * Comparing raw video counts from an expanded 10-query run against a narrow 2-query retrospective 12-item baseline produces artificial growth rates ($+250\%$ to $+2300\%$).
   * Therefore, the momentum and growth status are classified as **`"provisional"`** until a consistent, automated daily ingestion schedule accumulates longitudinal observations under an unchanging search matrix.

## 3. Limitations of Using YouTube Search as a Proxy for Fashion Popularity

FashionPulse AI utilizes YouTube Data API v3 as a primary social signal. However, analytical interpretation must account for key distinctions between search result frequency and broad cultural popularity:

1. **Upload Frequency vs. Consumer Adoption:**
   * A high volume of YouTube videos on a topic (e.g. *Vintage Streetwear & Y2K*) reflects **creator production frequency** and content creator supply, which often precedes or differs from mass consumer purchasing behavior.
2. **Search Query Bias:**
   * YouTube search results are conditioned by the configured search query terms. While the expanded 10-cluster query set covers broad apparel categories, trends outside the query matrix (e.g. niche subcultures not matching keywords) cannot be observed.
3. **Algorithm Relevance vs. Random Sampling:**
   * The YouTube `search.list` endpoint returns videos ranked by YouTube's internal relevance algorithm, which incorporates channel authority, title matching, and view velocity. It is not an unbiased random census of all uploads.
4. **Demographic & Format Specificity:**
   * YouTube fashion content skews toward lookbooks, hauls, styling tutorials, and capsule reviews. Fast-moving micro-aesthetics that emerge primarily through short-form loops (TikTok, Instagram Reels) or discussion threads (Reddit) may have different velocity characteristics.
5. **Mitigation Strategy:**
   * FashionPulse AI separates **Trend Score** (composite incorporating log-normalized engagement, classification confidence, and cross-platform presence) from **Momentum Score** (velocity), ensuring that high upload velocity alone does not override verified engagement and classification reliability.
